Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Expect gray water (Category 2) or black water (Category 3) in almost any flood, and with it bacteria, contamination and potentially hazardous substances that threaten your health. Day or night, 24/7, Largo Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout Largo to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

We start with fast water extraction, using truck-mounted and submersible pumps that can move thousands of gallons per hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Our crew returns daily, using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to track moisture until the structure is completely dry and mold growth is prevented. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Water pooled on any floor of the building, no matter where it came from
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below
  • Elevate appliances and electrical systems in flood-prone parts of your property
  • Consider flood insurance even if you are not in a designated flood zone
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation
